Yeti Rush — Privacy Policy

Effective 29 July 2026

Yeti Rush collects nothing, sends nothing and contains no advertising, no analytics and no third-party tracking of any kind. It has no user accounts and never asks who you are.

What the game stores

Your progress is saved on your own device and nowhere else. That is your coins, high score and best distance, which boards and riders you own and have equipped, your power-up upgrade levels, daily mission progress and the date of your last daily reward, and your sound, music and vibration settings.

It is written to the app's private storage using Android's standard preferences. Other apps cannot read it, we cannot read it, and uninstalling the game deletes it.

What leaves your device

Nothing. The game makes no network requests while you play — there is no server, no leaderboard and no cloud save. All artwork, sound and code are contained in the app itself, so it works identically in aeroplane mode.

Android's own backup

The app allows Android's system backup, so if you have Google backup switched on for your device, your saved progress may be included in it and restored when you set up a new phone. That backup is between you and Google under Google's privacy policy — the game has no access to it. You can turn it off in your device's backup settings.

Permissions

  • VIBRATE — the short buzz on a lane change, a pickup or a crash. Turn it off under Settings → Vibration.
  • INTERNET — required by the Android system component the game is drawn in, so that it can load the game's own files from inside the app. It is not used to contact anything on the internet.

The game does not request access to your location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos, files, or your advertising identifier.

Children

Yeti Rush is suitable for all ages. Because it collects no personal information from anyone, it collects none from children either, and it shows no advertising.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the new version is published at this address with a new effective date. A change that started collecting anything would also require an updated Data safety declaration on Google Play before the release could ship.
